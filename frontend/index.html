🎙️ 2-Minute Pitch for Examiners:

"Traditional evacuation models make two major mistakes: first, they naively sum up room populations to estimate exit loads. When corridors and rooms share doorways and thresholds, this double-counts evacuees by 20% to 35%, causing emergency dispatchers to falsely assume an exit is clogged and divert panicking crowds down longer paths. We solve this mathematically using the Principle of Inclusion–Exclusion (PIE).

Second, theoretical algorithms search for mathematical shortest-paths regardless of human cognitive panic. Drawing on Hick's Law, our model prioritizes UX-First Routing—favoring straight lines of sight and minimal turns. We only override the simpler route if the theoretical shortcut is faster by more than a configurable threshold (e.g. 15%)."

❓ Top Viva Questions & Answers

Q1: What is the exact formula for PIE across 3 overlapping zones?
|A ∪ B ∪ C| = |A| + |B| + |C| − (|A ∩ B| + |A ∩ C| + |B ∩ C|) + |A ∩ B ∩ C|

The pairwise intersections remove double-counted individuals, while the 3-way intersection adds back people subtracted one too many times.

Q2: Why does Hick's Law matter in an emergency?

Hick's Law states decision time increases logarithmically with the number of choices ($T = b \log_2(n + 1)$). In high-stress situations, every turn or branching hallway introduces hesitation, group splitting, and wrong turns. Real-world hesitation costs far more time than a few seconds saved by a complex theoretical shortcut.

Q3: How does the optimality threshold slider work?

If $\frac{T_{\text{UX}} - T_{\text{Optimal}}}{T_{\text{UX}}} > \text{Threshold}\%$, the system switches to the Optimal route. If the difference is small (e.g. 7%), the system stays on UX-First to prevent cognitive overload.
